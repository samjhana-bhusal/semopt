\begin{abstract}
Analytics over unstructured data increasingly relies on \emph{semantic operators}---
SQL-style \texttt{filter}, \texttt{map}, \texttt{extract}, \texttt{join}, and \texttt{rank}
whose predicates are evaluated by a large language model (LLM). The dominant cost is the
LLM calls themselves: running the strongest model on every row is often too expensive to
deploy. We present \textbf{semopt}, a query engine that executes semantic operators
through a cheap$\rightarrow$expensive model \emph{cascade} and escalates a row to a
stronger model only when the cheaper model's calibrated confidence is too low. Our central
contribution is that the escalation threshold is \emph{not} hand-tuned: it is derived by
\emph{weighted conformal calibration}, giving a distribution-free, finite-sample guarantee
that the end-to-end error rate stays at or below a user-specified target $\alpha$. On a
controlled synthetic workload, semopt holds empirical coverage within
\synthetic{$\pm 3\%$} of the nominal $1-\alpha$ across $\alpha \in \{0.05, 0.10, 0.20\}$
while costing \synthetic{$\approx 16\%$} of a naive strongest-model plan, and a cost-based
optimizer (reordering, predicate pushdown, semantic memoization) removes a further
\synthetic{$\approx 99\%$} of estimated cost versus an unoptimized plan. We describe the
system and evaluation protocol; validation on real workloads (Amazon Reviews, arXiv, CUAD)
is in progress.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Systems such as LOTUS~\cite{lotus}, Palimpzest~\cite{palimpzest}, and
DocETL~\cite{docetl} established semantic operators as a way to query unstructured data
with LLMs. The open problem they share is cost: an LLM call per row, at the strongest
model, does not scale. Model \emph{cascades}~\cite{frugalgpt} reduce this by trying a cheap
model first and escalating on low confidence, but they choose the escalation threshold
heuristically---so the accuracy that results is whatever the heuristic happens to produce.

We ask instead: \emph{can the user state the accuracy they need, and have the system
guarantee it at minimum cost?} semopt answers yes for the cascade's escalation decision, by
reusing weighted conformal prediction~\cite{tibshirani} to turn a labelled calibration set
into a threshold with a coverage guarantee. Our contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Conformal-guaranteed cascade escalation.} A principled alternative to
  threshold-heuristic cascades, with a finite-sample coverage guarantee that holds under
  bounded covariate shift (\S\ref{sec:conformal}).
  \item \textbf{A semantic-operator query optimizer} performing cost/selectivity
  reordering, LLM-predicate pushdown, and semantic memoization (\S\ref{sec:optimizer}).
  \item \textbf{An evaluation protocol and Pareto analysis} against five baselines, with a
  reproducible pipeline (\S\ref{sec:eval}).
\end{enumerate}
We do \emph{not} claim to solve the general semantic database; we contribute one optimizer
with one guarantee.

\section{System overview}
A \texttt{SemanticTable} exposes the five operators; each accepts a natural-language
predicate and a \texttt{target\_accuracy}. A query is built lazily, optimized, then
executed by a streaming operator tree. Every LLM-invoking operator may run through a
confidence cascade of model tiers (e.g. a local 1B model, a local 8B model, a frontier API
model). All calls are memoized in a persistent cache keyed by $(\textrm{model\_id},
\textrm{prompt hash})$, giving deterministic replay and cost containment.

\section{Conformal cascade}\label{sec:conformal}
At a tier we accept the cheap answer when its confidence $c \ge \tau$ and otherwise
escalate. Let a \emph{leaked error} be an accepted-but-wrong row. We choose the smallest
$\tau$ (maximising cheap acceptance) such that, on exchangeable test data,
\[
  P(\text{model wrong} \wedge c \ge \tau) \;\le\; \alpha .
\]
$\tau$ is the appropriate weighted quantile of the \emph{incorrect} calibration rows'
confidences, with an $(n{+}1)$ finite-sample correction; under covariate shift the
calibration points carry density-ratio weights $w(x)=p_{\text{test}}(x)/p_{\text{train}}(x)$,
following Tibshirani et al.~\cite{tibshirani}. For a two-tier cascade the end-to-end error
decomposes as
$\text{leaked}_0 + \Pr(\text{escalate}) \cdot \text{err}_{\text{strong}}$;
we budget the cheap tier at $\alpha_0 = \alpha - \Pr(\text{escalate})\cdot
\text{err}_{\text{strong}}$ and solve the resulting fixed point, so the total is controlled
at $\alpha$. Confidence itself is the max class probability from token logprobs, or a
self-consistency estimate when logprobs are unavailable.

\section{Query optimizer}\label{sec:optimizer}
The optimizer rewrites the plan with three passes: (i) \emph{reordering} adjacent filters
by $\text{cost}/(1-\text{selectivity})$ so cheap, selective predicates run first;
(ii) \emph{pushdown} of filters ahead of projections/maps they do not depend on; and
(iii) \emph{semantic memoization}, marking operators whose inputs contain duplicates.
Selectivity is estimated from a cheap-tier sample of the calibration set.

\section{Evaluation}\label{sec:eval}
\textbf{Protocol.} We report cost (\$ per 1k rows), task accuracy (F1 for filter),
empirical coverage with a 95\% bootstrap CI, and tier distribution. Baselines: naive
strongest (B0), naive cheapest (B1), hand-tuned FrugalGPT-style cascade (B2), fixed
threshold $\tau{=}0.8$ (B3), and our method; all share one cost model and cache.

\section{Limitations and status}
Results here are on a synthetic workload with a designed difficulty structure; they
validate the mechanism and protocol but are not evidence on real text. Real-workload
evaluation (Amazon Reviews complaint classification, arXiv triage, CUAD clause extraction)
is the immediate next step and requires a real model backend. The coverage guarantee is
marginal (not conditional) and assumes exchangeability or bounded, modelled covariate
shift; a workload that violates this is a finding, not a bug, and points toward the
non-exchangeable framework of Barber et al.~\cite{barber}.

\section{Conclusion}
semopt lets a user name the accuracy they need and pays only for it, by deriving cascade
escalation thresholds through weighted conformal calibration and rewriting the query to
cut LLM calls. On synthetic data the guarantee holds within $\pm3\%$ at a fraction of naive
cost; real-workload validation is in progress.
